Newsletter: November 18th, 2025
MIT IEEE/ACM is MIT's student branch of the International Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers and the Association of Computing Machinery.

Our organization aims to create and support a close community between students and faculty within the EECS department.

Welcome to those who recently signed up for our mailing list! Stay updated on all our events through this monthly newsletter.

IEEE/ACM Feedback

IEEE/ACM is hoping to learn more about our members and gather any feedback you may have to help us improve our organization and the types of events we host. We would really appreciate it if you could fill out this short survey.

Please feel free to reach out with any thoughts, feedback, or questions. Thank you so much for your time and input!

Opportunities with Jane Street

Grad Research Fellowship (GRF)

Applications for Jane Street's 2026 Graduate Research Fellowship (GRF) close next week for any interested PhD students!

Jane Street’s GRF supports exceptional doctoral students in electrical engineering, computer science, mathematics, physics, and statistics. Interested students can apply here and learn more about the program on their website. 

AMP Teaching Assistants

Jane Street is thrilled to share summer employment opportunities for our 2026 Academy of Math & Programming (AMP). AMP is a five-week academic summer program in New York City for recent high school graduates who have experienced barriers to access within advanced STEM educational experiences, and are interested in math and computer science. The program will take place from late June through late July.

They are hiring Math and Computer Science Teaching Assistants (TAs) to administer challenging and interactive courses and activities — including classes, problem-solving, and game playing — and supervise students as they spend their summer learning. The positions offer competitive compensation. Travel to and from the program, housing, and meals will be provided during AMP. 

Current undergraduate and graduate students can apply now!

AMP 2026: Teaching Assistants, Computer Science

AMP 2026: Teaching Assistants, Mathematics

Applications for the TA roles are assessed on a rolling basis. We encourage you to apply as soon as you are ready.  If you have any questions, please email academy@janestreet.com. 

Upcoming Events

In this edition of the newsletter, we have some exciting events planned that we'd love to share with you!

Dinner with smartSTAT

Join us for an exclusive dinner with Rohan Bajaj, co-founder of smartSTAT - a high-traction startup that won the HBS Shark Tank competition and secured funding from Kevin O’Leary - on Thursday, November 20th at 6:30 PM.

This will be a great opportunity to connect directly with smartSTAT and learn more about the company’s journey, product, and potential opportunities (please see the attached flyer for more information).

To attend, please fill out this form by Tuesday, November 18th and email your resume to Rohan Bajaj (RBAJAJ@smartstat.io). This event is co-hosted with MIT Biotech Group.

Further details will be shared with confirmed attendees.

Faculty Connections Event with Prof. Jelena Notaros

MIT IEEE/ACM is holding a faculty connections event with Professor Jelena Notaros on Thursday, November 20th at 10am in 32-124! The title of the talk is Silicon Photonics for LiDAR, Augmented Reality, Biophotonics, Quantum Systems, & Beyond. The talk will be followed by a Q&A session, and we will also have popup bagels. Sign up ASAP because the lottery closes tonight!

Jelena Notaros is an Assistant Professor of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, a Principal Investigator in the MIT Research Laboratory of Electronics, and a Core Faculty Member of the MIT Microsystems Technology Laboratories. She received her Ph.D. and M.S. degrees from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 2020 and 2017, respectively, and B.S. degree from the University of Colorado Boulder in 2015. Her research interests are in integrated silicon photonics devices, systems, and applications, with an emphasis on integrated optical phased arrays for LiDAR and augmented reality.

Sign up here!

Freshmen are especially encouraged to come and learn about researching on campus! Catered drinks and pastries will also be available. Be on the lookout for more information very soon. 

Thanksgiving Study Break

Faculty Connections Event with Prof. Adam Hartz

MIT IEEE/ACM is holding a faculty connections event/talk with Professor Adam Hartz on December 3. Prof. Hartz is a Senior Lecturer in EECS whose main interests lie in teaching engineering with an emphasis on problem-solving, engineering design, plan-making, and debugging. Notably, Prof. Hartz is highly involved in the design of Course 6 classes, including 6.101 (Fundamentals of Programming), 6.300 (Signal Processing), and 6.200 (Circuits and Electronics). He is also the developer behind CAT-SOOP, a programmable learning management system used in several subjects at MIT. Come to learn more about Prof. Hartz's technical work, instructional design, and his philosophy in structuring course 6 classes. Check your email for more information and the sign up form in the next couple of weeks. 

October Events Recap

General Body Meeting

We were happy to host our fall General Body Meeting to share upcoming events and discuss IEEE/ACM initiatives throughout the year. Students had the chance to learn about IEEE and socialize with fun games, dinner, and a raffle! 

LotSync CEO Session

We hosted lunch with Andy Short, the Founder and CEO of LotSync, an AI/ML company revolutionizing auto dealership operations with unified inventory intelligence and decision support. Andy also holds the position of General Manager at McGovern Hyundai.
At this event, students had an opportunity to learn more about entrepreneurship in the AI and operations space. Additionally, representatives from the EECS Alliance were present to discuss founder fellowships for students pursuing an MENG (6A-E track). Overall, we were happy to host meaningful conversations and networking opportunities for students interested in LotSync and entrepreneurship.

Faculty-Student Dinner with Prof. Andreea Bobu

In the next in our series of faculty-student events,  students had the opportunity to have dinner with Prof. Andreea Bobu, an assistant professor at MIT in AeroAstro and CSAIL. Prof. Bobu leads the Collaborative Learning and Autonomy Research Lab (CLEAR Lab), where she develops autonomous agents that learn to do tasks for, with, and around people. Prof. Bobu obtained her Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at UC Berkeley with Anca Dragan. Before MIT, she was also a Research Scientist at the AI Institute and an intern at NVIDIA in the Robotics Lab. Prior to her graduate degree, she received a B.S. in Computer Science at MIT. At this event, students learned all about her work in developing autonomous agents and ensuring that agents' behavior is consistent with human expectations, whether they interact with expert designers or novice users. 

Study Breaks

We hosted two study breaks in October to give students a chance to get food, have fun, and meet new people. One of the study breaks featured dinner and conversation, and the other event was a Halloween-themed study break featuring fall snacks/pastries, drinks, and pumpkin painting. We had a lot of fun connecting with the EECS community and giving students a chance to unwind during midterm season.
